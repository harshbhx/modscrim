Streamer docs. You run a Twitch channel and want your mods ready for a raid. Not what you came for? Find the right docs.

Sign in

Press Sign in with Twitch. Twitch shows its own consent screen with the permissions ModScrim needs and why. After you approve, the console shows your channel name, avatar and “Connected”.

ModScrim then reads your public blocked terms and AutoMod levels and starts the chat log. Step 1 of the console shows “Chat log: 0 messages, collecting since” the time you signed in. The false-ban check gets better as the log grows; until then ModScrim uses its banter pool.

Permissions

ModScrim asks for the smallest set of permissions the loop needs.

PermissionWhy ModScrim asksUsed in
moderator:read:blocked_termsRead your public blocked termsStep 1, Read filters
moderator:manage:blocked_termsAdd the terms you approve, and remove them when you press UndoStep 6, push and undo
moderator:read:automod_settingsRead your AutoMod levels. ModScrim never changes themStep 1, Read filters
moderation:readCheck attack lines against your filters with Check AutoMod Status, and see who your mods areStep 4 and mod sign-in
user:read:chat, user:bot, channel:botReceive your channel’s chat for the 7-day false-ban checkStep 1 and Step 5
user:write:chatPost one fixed line when a drill starts. Requested only if you turn the announcement onOptional

What stays private

  • Your Twitch tokens are encrypted at rest and used only on the server. They never reach a browser.
  • Twitch doesn’t share your private blocked terms with apps, so ModScrim can only see and test the public ones.

Disconnect

Use Sign out and delete my data to remove your tokens, chat log, drills, reports and chat subscription. If you remove ModScrim from your Twitch connections instead, ModScrim deletes the same data once Twitch tells it about the change. See remove everything.

If the console says “Reconnect Twitch”, your token needs refreshing: sign in again.
