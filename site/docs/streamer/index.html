Streamer docs. You run a Twitch channel and want your mods ready for a raid. Not what you came for? Find the right docs.

The idea

A practice chat floods with generated scam, spam and bot lines written to slip past your channel’s own blocked terms, mixed with harmless banter. Your mods race to remove the attacks. Then Twitch grades your filters, a rule finder proposes up to six new blocked terms, and the terms are proven on a fresh wave of attacks with zero matches against your real chat. You push them to Twitch with one click, and you can undo.

What a drill gives you

  • Trained mods. Each mod gets caught, false bans and average reaction time.
  • A number for your filters. “9 of 14 attack lines would get past your filters”, graded by Twitch’s Check AutoMod Status. The 9 of 14 is an example; yours comes from your own channel.
  • Rules you can trust. Each proposed term is checked against your last 7 days of chat, so it can’t ban a regular.

What it never does

  • It never posts attack text to your chat. The only message it can send is one optional fixed line when a drill starts, and it is off unless you turn it on.
  • It never changes your AutoMod settings. It only reads them.
  • It never adds a blocked term without your approval, and every push can be undone.

Every generated link ends in .invalid, so it can’t open. Read the full list on the safety page.

How long it takes

The drill itself runs 30 seconds to 3 minutes. From the first whistle to the report is usually under six minutes. Setup takes about three minutes, and nothing needs installing.

Start here

Follow the quick start, or try a demo room first. No Twitch account is needed for the demo, and its grades are a labelled local estimate.
